## Introduction: A Starry Sky with a Price Tag

By the end of the 22nd century, humanity had finally stepped out of its cradle. Yet, just as every inch of Earth had been carved up by capital at the dawn of the Industrial Revolution, deep-space exploration could not escape the fate of monopolization.

By this time, Earth had long since become a massive prison—depleted of resources and drowning in debt. The sky was sliced ​​into fragments by a dense web of commercial satellites and the private space stations of super-conglomerates. For the vast majority of the working class, gazing at the stars was no longer a source of poetic romance, but a cruel luxury.

Legitimate slots for Mars colonization, perfect eco-pods built from nanomaterials, cutting-edge AI systems running around the clock, and clean synthetic food—all bore astronomical price tags. These commodities belonged exclusively to the tiny elite who held monopoly capital and financial privileges on Earth.

While the law nominally declared that "space belongs to all humanity," in reality, the guns and surveillance satellites of the UN Space Agency (UNSA) were trained only on the aristocrats who could afford the exorbitant space taxes. As for the common people? Earth’s scrap heaps were their ultimate destination.

---

Part I: The Black Market and the "Tin Can"

It was against this suffocating backdrop that an underground black-market network known as the "Ghost Passage" quietly took root in the depths of the dark web.

An anonymous billionaire—nearing death and harboring a deep hatred for corporate monopolies—exhausted his remaining private assets to secretly assemble an unofficial Mars expedition vessel via the black market. It was an act of illegal stowaway travel that flagrantly violated interstellar law.

Because funds had dwindled significantly through layers of dark-web transactions, the vessel could not utilize any expensive, state-of-the-art aerospace equipment. Most of its structure—from the outer carbon-fiber armor to the internal air circulation pumps—consisted of second-hand parts scavenged from scrapyards and bankrupt factories in Earth's major heavy-industrial cities. This is a "Wasteland Ark" cobbled together from scraps. It lacks a sleek, streamlined profile; instead, it features rough weld seams, peeling yellow industrial paint, and the constant risk of catastrophic structural failure due to pressure imbalances.

Yet, it has been packed with a group of desperate "stowaways" with nowhere else to turn.

---

Part Two: Seven Exiled Souls

This expedition vessel—named the *Freedom* (though it is, in reality, a dilapidated wreck)—is crowded with seven outcasts rejected by mainstream society or driven to the brink by debt and despair:

1. **Schmidt (German Engineer):** A former senior technician for a European aerospace giant; blacklisted across the industry for exposing corporate corruption, he embarks on this point-of-no-return journey fueled by rage and a fanatical obsession with machinery.
2. **Sato (Japanese Hardware Expert):** A taciturn underground modification fanatic with a disabled right hand; he can weld two pieces of scrap metal into a functional engine without a single blueprint.
3. **Leo (Young American Surveyor):** Burdened by astronomical student loans and credit card debt, he was lured here by a black-market ad and now suffers from severe "color deprivation syndrome" and mental instability.
4. **Dr. Elena (Russian Botanist):** An exiled scientist who conducted extreme agricultural experiments in Siberia; she has smuggled a large stash of illegal genetically modified crop seeds in her luggage.
5. **Dr. Marcus (British Ex-Plastic Surgeon):** A hypocrite who lost his medical license and went bankrupt after a disastrous high-stakes gamble; he fled to Mars to escape debt collectors and is secretly hoarding the ship's last supply of sedatives.
6. **Jax (Australian Hacker):** A rebel with a background in deep-sea oil rig communications and an expert in signal interception; his favorite pastime is spamming UNSA surveillance frequencies.
7. **Mateo (Pakistani Heavy Machinery Mechanic):** A tough guy from the illegal underground tunnels of the Amazon; he handles the dirtiest, most grueling manual labor.

None of these seven individuals is a hero in the traditional sense. They were selfish, fragile, and deeply flawed, yet in the face of a desperate plight, they gritted their teeth and fought to carve a future out of the Martian wasteland.

Part Three: The Protagonist — A Rusted Tin Can and the Ghost of an Old Worker

To comply with a cold, rigid UNSA regulation—"All long-haul deep-space manned capsules must be equipped with a psychological support and crisis management robot"—this destitute group of stowaways purchased the cheapest model available at a scrapyard.

It was a heavy-duty industrial robot with a battered exterior that reeked of low-grade machine oil and moved on heavy tank treads; its model designation was **`UNIT 74`**.

Yet, deep within its damaged neural network core—held together in places by nothing more than electrical tape—lay a secret unknown even to the counterfeiters who sold it:
Its foundational logic matrix was a digital imprint of the consciousness of an elderly Chinese engineer named **Han Yuanshan**.

During his life on Earth, Han Yuanshan had spent twenty years as a welder at a heavy machinery plant on the brink of collapse. He smoked cheap cigarettes, swore constantly, and had a fiery temper, holding nothing but contempt for the windbags of office politics. But he possessed hands scarred by countless burns from high-temperature arc welding, and he knew how to bring a machine back from the dead using nothing but a piece of wire and a wrench just before it was consigned to the scrap heap.

When `UNIT 74`—a fusion of an old worker's soul and a dilapidated mechanical shell—was activated, it found itself not in a high-tech laboratory, but amidst the suffocating, toxic perchlorate dust and sub-zero temperatures of Mars, alongside seven Earth elites on the verge of a mental breakdown.

---

Part Four: Dark Humor on the Martian Wasteland

Humanity’s romanticized vision of the cosmos was shattered the moment they set foot on Mars.

There were no oases, no alien ruins—only endless gales, a dark-red rocky wilderness, and lethal radiation capable of piercing their shields at any moment. The base’s airlocks would crack and split in the biting cold; the water circulation system would let out a despair-inducing shriek at the mere touch of a speck of Martian dust; and the air was perpetually thick with the stench of rust and sweat.

Yet, against this backdrop of a brutally unforgiving wasteland, absurd dark humor became the fuel that kept these people alive.

When Schmidt, the haughty European engineer, screamed in frustration over a power grid overload, "UNIT 74"—a machine coated in peeling yellow paint and leaking hydraulic fluid—would lumber over, exhaling a phantom smoke ring with a rasping, metallic voice:
*"Stop your howling, Schmidt. Back when I was training apprentices in a crummy factory on Earth, I dealt with messes far worse than this busted circuit. Whining won't fix an air leak—hand me that roll of electrical tape."*

There are no grand interstellar epics here, no sweeping narratives about saving the world.
This is the story of a band of exiles—cast aside by their times—who, armed with nothing but shoddy second-hand gear, a foul-mouthed attitude, and a burning, resilient humanity, carved a home out of the desolate wilderness on a cold, lonely red planet.
